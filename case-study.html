Bodywise: a clearer starting point

Product case study · Pankhuri Srivastava · 22 October 2026

Give women a useful next step across Bodywise’s range through a focused, understandable assessment. Earn conversion through relevance and trust. Targets: at least 80% assessment completion and 20% purchase among valid submitters. These are proposed success criteria, not demonstrated results.

1. What I would deliver

A responsive landing page, working assessment, editable answer review and personalised report. The accompanying PM model covers care routing, report structure, commercial plans, measurable hypotheses, experiments and a prioritised backlog. Checkout and real medical services are outside this prototype’s scope.

The prototype is an independent case-study concept, not an official Bodywise service. It collects no contact details, stores answers only in tab memory, and does not prescribe or sell. Refreshing clears answers. Print/save-to-PDF uses the browser.

2. Evidence and limits

The supplied Man Matters landing page was accessible with the supplied credentials. Observed structure: strong promise; problem education; a system explained through scan, blood, doctor, plan and tracking; four steps; reassurance; FAQs; repeated assessment CTAs. Its primary CTA opened a “page not available” screen during inspection. The exact reference question sequence could not be verified.

The supplied ten-page report provides the reference for the output experience. Its sequence is personal summary/stage; estimated drivers; comparison with other users; mechanism education; products mapped to jobs; “You said / What we do / What to change”; clinician and app support; timeline; evidence; monthly plan and FAQ. These are observations of the reference, not independently verified efficacy claims.

The report also illustrates risks to avoid: a hormonal cause sounds established before clinician confirmation, percentages imply precision that the inputs may not support, and the example says photos were skipped while displaying a section framed around photo-based comparison. The printed distribution shows 4% at the user’s stage and 97% further along, which requires reconciliation. We should preserve explanation and continuity without importing unsupported precision.

Bodywise’s current navigation groups the catalogue into Hair, Body Care, Health & Fitness, Sun Protection and Face. Periods/PCOS and intimate-care entries also appear on its sitemap/legacy pages, but those listings are not proof of current stock or service availability. In the prototype, these remain clinician-navigation routes. They need catalogue-owner validation before commercial launch. Gummies are a format, not a separate health concern.

3. Product decision

Cover the range at entry; focus the journey after entry. A complete catalogue should not require a complete-body questionnaire. Ask for one priority, then show a relevant concern branch and a report that uses the answers. Let users start a second concern later; in production, retain their chosen age/context only with appropriate consent.

Initial audience assumption: adult women in India arriving from Bodywise campaigns or the site, often on mobile, with an existing concern and uncertainty about what to do next. Traffic intent, language mix, device mix, clinical-routing rate and baseline conversion are unknown. Validate these before forecasting revenue.

Women’s context should affect suitability rather than serve as a cosmetic rebrand: pregnancy/trying to conceive, postpartum/breastfeeding, medicines and existing conditions, cycle-related questions only when relevant, private wording, and the ability to decline sensitive context. Do not assume that women’s hair loss has the same cause or treatment as men’s hair loss. Do not infer PCOS from acne or irregular periods.

4. User flow and question design

Landing → explanation/acknowledgement → age → priority category → branch concern → facial skin type (face only) → duration → impact → relevant context → safety question → product suitability → current routine → preference → review → submit → report.

There are ten questions on the usual adult path and eleven for face care, which also asks skin type. Selecting concerning symptoms displays immediate medical-help guidance and shortens the path before product-fit, routine and preference questions (seven questions, or eight for face care). Branch concern options change by category; safety wording changes for period and intimate symptoms. Back and edit preserve answers. Changing the category invalidates the previous concern answer. A short under-18 path produces an age-appropriate next-step summary rather than an adult plan. Production analytics must report this route separately so it cannot disguise poor adult-path completion.

The acknowledgement explains purpose and local demo handling. A production health-data consent and retention policy must be designed and reviewed before collection. Report access is not conditional on marketing consent or phone verification. An optional save/send feature would come after report value and need separate opt-in.

5. Report and care-plan model

Use the reference’s explanatory spine: what you said → what it can tell us → what we do next. Keep the summary visible first; deeper explanation can expand underneath.

  1. Personal starting point: priority, concern, duration and impact; date/report version in production. No numerical cause attribution.
  2. Evidence from answers: quote the specific answer before interpreting it. Separate self-reported facts from hypotheses and unknowns.
  3. Meaning and limits: educational explanation reviewed for the selected concern. Explicitly state when examination or testing could be needed.
  4. Relevant context: life stage, medicines, irritation and preferences change the next step. Missing context is not treated as “no risk.”
  5. A manageable next step: one essential action, optional additions only when justified, and the reason for each. A non-product action is valid.
  6. Expectations and review: category-specific guidance approved by clinicians. No guaranteed result dates or universal six-month timeline.
  7. Care ownership: “not reviewed” until actual clinician review; then show the real reviewer, qualifications, registration where applicable, and review timestamp. No placeholder doctor badge.
  8. Appropriate CTA: explore an eligible routine, arrange an available consultation, or seek prompt care. Keep the report usable without purchase.

Rule precedence and implementation contract

Under-18 exclusion → prompt-care symptoms → clinician-review conditions/unknowns → category restrictions → routine eligibility. Urgent symptoms always override ordinary commercial preferences on adult paths. The under-18 prototype does not collect symptoms and only offers age-appropriate professional guidance.

Clinician review is required for pregnancy/trying to conceive, postpartum/breastfeeding, medicines/conditions, declined context, uncertain safety answers, severe daily impact, periods/intimate routes, irritation, or a request for an expert. Hair shedding/thinning/dandruff and sleep/low-energy paths also require review in this conservative prototype. No clinical cause is assigned.

Production rule record: rule ID, rule version, trigger answers, route, approved explanation, allowed product classes, prohibited ingredients/classes, escalation copy, approving clinician and review date. Keep an audit of the rule version that produced each report. Clinical governance is a launch prerequisite; commercial experiments cannot relax clinical eligibility.

For an eligible dry-body-skin example, the report could propose a gentle cleansing/moisturising foundation, then explain optional product choices. For a period-irregularity example, it should explain what to record for a clinician discussion, without diagnosing PCOS or selling a “hormone balance” fix. For postpartum shedding, contextual review precedes any treatment suggestion.

SKU matching, when built, must intersect clinical eligibility, concern fit, allergies, existing routine, inventory, price/affordability and user preference. Deduplicate overlapping actives and products. Do not automatically stack face, body and hair bundles. If no suitable SKU is available, preserve useful advice and show an honest no-product outcome. Budget cannot override suitability.

6. Define the targets before optimising them

Use a stable, privacy-appropriate anonymous first-party identity, deduplicate retries, and exclude internal QA/bots by a preregistered rule. Do not redefine denominators after results arrive.

  • Assessment start: first assessment question successfully rendered after explicit Begin. Track landing CTA click and acknowledgement abandonment separately.
  • Valid submission: required answers accepted and report successfully generated. In production, acceptance should be server-confirmed; an HTTP error is not completion. Repeated submits count once.
  • Completion: unique starters with valid submission within 24 hours / unique starters. Keep all route outcomes in the aggregate and publish adult, under-18, prompt-care and clinician-review breakdowns. Also show landing-CTA-to-submit to expose pre-start friction.
  • Purchase: unique valid submitters making at least one paid, non-test order attributable to the assessment within seven days / all unique valid submitters. Count people, not orders, and do not remove referred users from this denominator. Report gross paid and net-of-cancellations/refunds separately; use net retained purchasers for the business decision. Proposed maturity rule: an order must be paid within seven days of submission, then observed for 30 days after payment before calling the purchaser retained. Confirm the follow-up horizon with the business before launch; a seven-day paid metric cannot be presented as a mature retained metric.
  • Attribution: first submitted report anchors the seven-day window; connect report ID to checkout/order only with appropriate data handling. Report cross-device/unmatched loss instead of assuming it converted.

The 20% target assumes “form submitter” means a completed assessment, not a captured phone lead. Confirm this with Kumar/Ayush before interpreting results. If lead capture is required, publish assessment-submit, consented-lead and lead-to-purchase metrics separately. They are different denominators.

Purchase window and attribution contract

Window: start at the server-accepted timestamp of a person's first valid assessment submission in the experiment, submitted_at_utc. Include orders whose first successful captured payment satisfies submitted_at_utc <= paid_at_utc < submitted_at_utc + 168 hours. The window uses elapsed time in UTC, not seven calendar-date labels. An order created in the window but paid outside it does not qualify. A later retry cannot rewrite the first captured-payment timestamp.

Original assessment: create an immutable random assessment_id and persist the accepted submission in a restricted first-party service. Enrol each known person once per experiment using their earliest accepted submission. Repeated submissions and later concern assessments can have new IDs but must point to the original cohort_assessment_id; they cannot restart the clock, reassign the experiment variant or add another person to the denominator. Resolve known identity duplicates using a predeclared deterministic rule and audit revisions. This is a proposed production contract; anonymous people across unlinked devices cannot always be recognised.

Metric names and denominators:

Metric Numerator Denominator / reporting rule
Seven-day attributed paid conversion Unique enrolled submitters with at least one merchant-verified paid, non-test order linked to their original assessment and captured inside the 168-hour window All unique valid submitters in that enrolment cohort, including clinician-review and no-product outcomes; one person counts once regardless of number of orders
Seven-day attributed retained conversion, 30-day follow-up Those submitters with at least one qualifying order not fully cancelled or fully refunded within 30 days after payment Same all-submitter denominator; partial refunds remain a purchaser but reduce reported net revenue. For consistency with the conservative funnel, the case study's 160/800 = 20% refers to retained purchasers. Its 178/800 = 22.25% is the seven-day paid rate. Do not call both rates 20%, compare a paid-only result with the retained target, or treat the retained rate as mature before the follow-up ends. The business must confirm whether the assignment's 20% target means paid or retained conversion before a real experiment; this case study explicitly uses retained conversion as the stricter business ambition.

Attribution path: original assessment record → product-link redirect/reference → merchant checkout metadata → order/payment record → reconciliation of cancellations/refunds. Use an opaque signed/validated reference tied to the original assessment; store the verified ID in cart/checkout and order metadata, so it survives later navigation. Do not put symptoms, report contents, contact details or health-category labels in URL parameters or general-purpose analytics. UTMs can describe acquisition but are not proof that an order belongs to an assessment.

What counts as a verified link: either the merchant order carries a reference validated against the assessment service, or an authenticated first-party identity match deterministically connects that purchaser to the original assessment under the approved data-use policy. Record which method was used. The assessment cohort remains original-assessment-based even if a later ad, reminder or direct visit closes the purchase; record those marketing touches separately. Do not infer a match solely from a click, timestamp proximity, IP address or similar device fingerprint.

Cross-device and unmatched orders: support an authenticated account or explicit saved-report handoff where available and consented. If the person cannot be reliably connected across devices, label the order unmatched and exclude it from the attributed numerator. Keep the submitter in the original denominator; do not discard unmatched users to inflate conversion or assume unmatched orders converted. Report the measured rate as attributable conversion with a disclosed coverage limitation, not a complete measure of all assessment-influenced purchases.

Payment and duplicate handling: ingest server-to-server merchant payment/order confirmations, validate the sender, deduplicate event IDs/order IDs, and reconcile against the merchant order ledger. Browser success-page views and outbound product clicks never count as purchases. A repeat buyer can place multiple qualifying orders, but is counted once in the purchaser numerator; each order belongs to only one original assessment cohort under this contract.

Cohort maturity and late data: paid conversion is provisional until every included submission has had its full 168 hours plus an agreed ingestion-lag allowance. Retained conversion is provisional until each qualifying order has had its full 30-day follow-up. Publish an as-of timestamp and cohort dates; a retained cohort may require up to 37 days from its final submission, plus ingestion lag. Use merchant event time for eligibility, not webhook arrival time. A late webhook for an in-window payment can update the cohort through the stated backfill policy, with an audit trail; it does not change the purchase window.

Minimum event/data fields: assessment_id, cohort_assessment_id, restricted pseudonymous person/account key where available, experiment_id, assigned variant, submitted_at_utc, merchant cart/order ID, first captured paid_at_utc, payment status, reference validation status/link method, cancellation/refund events, event ID, and ingestion timestamp. Maintain a separate ledger for gross amount, partial/full refunds and net revenue. Scope access and retention to the approved purposes.

Before accepting the conversion number, QA must demonstrate: an in-window order links to the original submission; exactly 168 hours is excluded; an order created before the deadline but paid later is excluded; a duplicate webhook and a second order do not add another purchaser; a second quiz does not restart the clock; a verified cross-device match joins correctly; an unmatched order remains unmatched; an in-window late payment event backfills by event time; and a full cancellation/refund removes an otherwise qualifying retained purchaser when no other qualifying retained order remains. Reconcile the attributed numerator to merchant records and publish cohort/sample counts with uncertainty intervals.

Implementation status: none of the server-side attribution, merchant webhooks, identity reconciliation or purchase analytics described here exists in the static prototype. The current website provides product links only. This contract must be implemented and verified with Bodywise's merchant/analytics team before claiming measured progress toward the 20% target.

7. How we will test the 20% purchase target

The target is an ambition. The funnel is a hypothesis tree. The experiment programme is how we decide whether that ambition is achievable. There are no observed baseline rates, experiment results or measured uplifts yet. The prototype currently sends users to Bodywise product pages and has no merchant checkout/order integration. An outbound product click is not a checkout start or a purchase. Until merchant events can be joined to assessments with appropriate consent and access, the 20% outcome cannot be measured reliably.

For the conservative everyday-care scenario, with clinician-route purchases initially assumed zero:

Purchase per submitter = eligible share × plan-engagement rate × checkout-start rate × paid rate × retained rate.

The illustrative requirement is 75% × 80% × 60% × (178/288) × (160/178) = 20%. Paid and retained rates use the exact scenario counts; displayed table percentages are rounded. These factors identify testable dependencies, not evidence that any factor will reach its assumed value. Appropriate clinical routing is a constraint, not a conversion lever to relax.

Define each transition so the experiments are interpretable

  • Submission → eligible route: clinician-approved rules assign eligibility. Record a non-sensitive operational route code under restricted access; do not infer safety from purchase intent.
  • Eligible report → plan engagement: the user explicitly opens a “View my routine” control. Log product-section visibility separately as plan_exposure; auto-rendering a card must not count as intentional plan engagement. This is a proposed production measurement contract, not an event currently collected by the prototype.
  • Plan engagement → checkout start: the merchant confirms entry to a cart/checkout with a joinable assessment identifier. Product-page outbound clicks remain a distinct intermediate event. Without merchant confirmation, label this stage unmeasured.
  • Checkout start → paid order: merchant order/payment confirmation, deduplicated by purchaser and order ID. A redirect to a success page is not sufficient by itself.
  • Paid → retained purchaser: reconcile cancellations/refunds at a fixed follow-up horizon. Keep payment failure, cancellation and return reasons separate so different problems do not collapse into “conversion.”

How stage data change the backlog

First priority is measurement readiness (G3), then the observed, actionable bottleneck. The initial RICE order is a provisional implementation sequence, not a fixed experiment roadmap. For each candidate, estimate:

Incremental retained purchases ≈ users entering the affected stage × plausible absolute improvement in that stage × observed downstream conversion.

Then compare this benefit range with confidence, implementation effort, clinical constraints and margin. Use measured downstream conversion and eligible reach when available. Do not add together experiment uplifts: interventions can overlap, change the audience mix or interact.

To show why stage location matters, the following is a sensitivity calculation using only the original scenario. It assumes each stage could independently improve by ten percentage points, with every other factor and the audience held fixed. These are not uplift predictions or equally feasible interventions:

Hypothetical isolated change Additional retained purchases per 1,000 original starters Interpretation
Plan engagement 80% → 90% 20.0 600 eligible submitters × .10 × .60 × (160/288)
Checkout start 60% → 70% 26.7 480 plan-engaged users × .10 × (160/288)
Checkout-to-retained conversion 55.6% → 65.6% 28.8 288 checkout starters × .10; the gain could come from payment or retention and must be diagnosed

This does not establish that checkout work should always rank first. A ten-point improvement might be implausible there, cost more, or have weaker supporting evidence. It shows why “the largest drop-off” is insufficient: ranking depends on recoverable loss, downstream value, cost and confidence.

Illustrative reprioritisation example, not observed data: if deliberate plan engagement is already 85%, merchant-confirmed checkout is 40%, and cost feedback is common, move E3/B3 ahead of E2/B2. If checkout intent is strong but payment failures dominate, move E4/B4 first. If orders are paid but frequently cancelled, move E5/B10 ahead of further acquisition or checkout optimisation. If current attribution cannot distinguish these cases, G3 stays first.

Decision rules and cadence

  1. Establish baseline cohorts with raw numerators/denominators, uncertainty intervals and source/device/route mix. Choose the minimum useful effect, experiment size and guardrail limits before assigning traffic. Do not select thresholds after seeing results.
  2. Use one stage-specific primary metric to diagnose the intervention, with retained purchase per submitter and per starter as cross-funnel outcomes. Treat upstream wins as provisional until the purchase window and retention follow-up mature.
  3. Ship only when the agreed benefit criterion is met and suitability, customer outcomes, margin, consent and technical guardrails remain acceptable. A stage win with worse final outcomes is not a funnel win.
  4. A well-powered null result should lower the confidence/impact estimate for that hypothesis. An inconclusive result calls for more data or a different test, not a claim of success. Investigate harm or a safety incident immediately.
  5. At each weekly PM/analytics review, refresh eligible reach, downstream rates, evidence-based impact ranges and effort in the backlog. Clinical owners review routing/suitability changes; commerce/operations owners review price, checkout and fulfilment evidence. Account for the different outcome-maturity windows before reranking.

The defensible claim is therefore: we have a measurable path to test the 20% ambition, and explicit evidence that would change our approach; we do not yet have evidence that the product will achieve 20%.

Measurement and validation plan

First run 8–12 moderated sessions across hair, skin/body, wellness and sensitive-concern users, including mobile and lower-bandwidth conditions. Validate category understanding, privacy comprehension and whether users understand that the report is not a diagnosis. This is qualitative research, not evidence of an 80% rate.

Then establish baseline counts with the same denominators and source/device mix. Instrument: landing_view, assessment_cta_click, acknowledgement_continue, assessment_start, question_view, question_answered, answer_edited, submit_attempt, assessment_submitted, report_view, route_assigned, plan_exposure, plan_view (explicit engagement), product_outbound_click, merchant-confirmed checkout_start, payment_attempt, payment_failed, order_paid, order_cancelled/refunded. These are proposed production events, not analytics already operating in the prototype. Include pseudonymous assessment ID, experiment/variant, event ID, timestamp, step ID, technical result and rule version. Do not put raw symptom answers, cycle details or phone numbers in general-purpose analytics. Any route metadata still requires minimisation and access review.

Report completion by source, device, route, age eligibility and new/returning visitor. Separate technical drop-off from voluntary exit and appropriate care navigation. Use purchase-per-starter as a cross-funnel check so an experiment cannot improve completion by attracting unqualified submits.

For A/B tests, randomise at the starter/person level, choose one primary metric and predefine the minimum useful effect, confidence level, power, guardrails and stopping rule. As an illustrative sizing check, a 75%→80% completion change needs roughly 1,100 users per arm, and a 15%→20% purchase change roughly 900 submitters per arm at 5% two-sided alpha and 80% power. Recalculate with measured baselines, add allowance for attribution loss, and allow the full seven-day purchase window to mature. Do not run experiments sequentially on the same people without handling carryover.

A point estimate of 80% or 20% does not establish that the true rate is at least the target. Report uncertainty intervals; agree whether success requires the lower interval bound above the target or an operational point-estimate threshold before launch. Inspect safety incidents immediately regardless of the statistical stopping rule.

8. Prioritised backlog

Framework: mandatory safety/data prerequisites first, then RICE. Reach is users exposed per 1,000 assessment starters, Impact is a directional multiplier, Confidence is 0–1, Effort is estimated person-weeks. Score = Reach × Impact × Confidence / Effort. These are explicit planning estimates, to replace after baseline research; they are not measured uplift or additive benefits. Use the stage-specific evidence and decision rules in Section 7 to rerank this list. G3 is the first commercial-learning prerequisite: without attribution, the 20% target cannot be evaluated.

Delivery sequence

Phase 1 — validate and establish: agree target denominators, audit the reference flow when restored, validate catalogue and clinical rules, interview users, finish G1–G4. Deliver the clickable prototype and measurement specification.

Phase 2 — release a measured core: implement B1/B2; run an instrumented pilot covering each route. Release commercial routine selection only when B3 and fulfilment/suitability checks pass. The production system needs durable, secure submissions and clinically approved content beyond this local-memory demo.

Phase 3 — improve purchase: B3/B4 and observed B5 issues; test report and price levers with a mature seven-day outcome window. Diagnose drop-off rather than run all experiments at once.

Phase 4 — expand only from evidence: save/reminder, integrated care navigation and second concerns based on the observed bottleneck and capacity. Review margin and retained conversion alongside acquisition costs and care outcomes.

11. Product recommendation implementation

The report now includes specific products rather than only category links. The catalogue is a snapshot checked 22 October 2026 across 125 reachable official Bodywise product listings. Of 81 Shop All listings, 78 pass metadata and availability checks for matching; the rest remain catalogue references. Individual product pages take precedence over category listings where prices differ. Links, prices, pack sizes, relevant ingredients and matching reasons are visible on cards; the researched library is available at Product library. No product rating, clinical success rate, guaranteed outcome or monthly cost is invented.

Routine routes show one starting product with optional complementary care where appropriate. Expandable alternatives and grouped pack/fragrance variants support comparison. Kits replace overlapping individual items and are excluded from the starting estimate. Additional product pages, out-of-stock entries and conflicting metadata do not enter recommendations. Relevant optional products stay visible for all preferences with an “Optional recommendation” label and a short explanation. They stay excluded from the starting estimate until explicitly selected. An optional checkbox changes the pack-price estimate; it does not create a cart or subscription. Users purchase only by navigating to the official store. Shipping, discounts, stock and full suitability require confirmation there.

The oily-skin occasional-breakout path selects the 2% face wash, while combination skin selects the 1% option. Dry/normal skin prioritises moisturising rather than assuming an acid cleanser is suitable; sensitive/uncertain skin needs individual review. Current acids, retinoids, prescribed treatments, ingredient allergies or irritation hold automatic suggestions. Supplement and hair/scalp treatment paths can show clearly labelled options for a clinician conversation on otherwise unrestricted paths. Pregnancy, breastfeeding/postpartum, medical/medicine context, uncertain safety, severe symptoms, under-18, periods and intimate-care routes hold product suggestions.

Product photos remain brand assets linked from its image host. Prices are dated snapshots rather than live feeds. Before production, replace the snapshot with maintained catalogue/stock data, complete clinical rules, review the wording and instrument outbound clicks and purchase attribution appropriately.
